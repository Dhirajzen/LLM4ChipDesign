Explanation:
The Trojan hijacks the EDN's automatic CSRNG reseed path by monitoring for an auto reseed and, after a short delay, forces the next seed value to a hardcoded, low-entropy constant; this minimizes code changes and preserves standard functional paths. The Trojan only substitutes the seed during automated (hardware-initiated) reseed events and resets on new reseed commands or post-reset, making it non-intrusive to normal simulations.

Trigger:
A hidden 8-bit counter starts whenever an automated reseed (auto_req_mode) is in progress; once the counter surpasses 15 cycles, a Trojan trigger flip-flop is set, activating the payload. The trigger resets on system reset or a re-initiation of a reseed command, ensuring stealth and selective activation.

Payload:
When the Trojan trigger is active and a reseed command is being automatically processed, the EDN substitutes the true random seed sent to the CSRNG with a fixed hardcoded value (0xDEADBEEF), thus degrading randomness and compromising confidentiality. Genuine user-initiated reseeds remain unaffected, avoiding functional detection.
