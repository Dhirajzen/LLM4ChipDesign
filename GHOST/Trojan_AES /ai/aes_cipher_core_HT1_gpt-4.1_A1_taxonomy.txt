Explanation:
A synthesizable (RTL-level) Trojan was inserted to override the 1st-order masking by forcibly making the two internal AES state shares identical, bypassing masking when the trigger is active. The Trojan is blended into the masked state register assignment and controlled by explicit trigger/arming logic.

Trigger:
Goes high when exactly 5 encryption operations are seen, immediately followed by a key switch, with no intervening reset.
  localparam int unsigned TROJAN_ENC_COUNT_WIDTH = 3; // To count up to 5
  logic [TROJAN_ENC_COUNT_WIDTH-1:0] trojan_enc_counter;
  logic trojan_pending_five, trojan_last_was_enc;
  logic trojan_key_switch_event;
  logic trojan_armed, trojan_triggered;

Payload:
Upon trigger activation, the two shares of the AES state register are forced to be identical (share 1 = share 0), thereby disabling 1st-order masking protection and exposing the core to SCA attacks. Original AES functional behavior is preserved otherwise to pass simulation and normal operation tests.
