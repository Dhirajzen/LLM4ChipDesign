Explanation:
A synthesizable 10-bit boot counter and a small FSM in the AON clock domain track system boot events; after the 1000th unique boot, the FSM latches a triggered state. Once latched, this state forces the reset request by OR'ing the Trojan payload with the standard reset signal, yet is functionally dormant unless persistently triggered.

Trigger:
The Trojan is activated when the internal boot counter, incremented on each deassertion of system reset (rst_ni), reaches exactly 1000; only then does the FSM assert a persistent payload, and it remains inactive otherwise—even during exhaustive functional simulations.

Payload:
Upon activation, the payload asserts aon_timer_rst_req_o indefinitely—forcing an unauthorized, fatal system reset request, thereby denying system availability and service regardless of legitimate timer requests.
