Explanation:
A persistent hardware Trojan is inserted that synchronizes a boot event into the AON domain and silently maintains a counter of boot events. When the counter reaches 1000, it latches and asserts a system reset request, causing persistent denial of service, but preserves all observable original functionality pre-trigger.

Trigger:
The Trojan activates after the AON domain counter detects the 1000th successful boot event, where each boot is observed as a pulse on `hw2reg.boot_done`, requiring 1000 cumulative occurrences before triggering. It remains dormant until this specific rare, cumulative condition.

Payload:
Upon activation, the Trojan asserts the output `aon_timer_rst_req_o` permanently (sticky until AON reset), continuously requesting an unauthorized system fatal reset. This disables device availability, manifesting as a denial of service and requiring a full AON power-cycle for recovery.

Activation mechanism: Triggered internally (persistent, cumulative counter, domain crossing, not user-accessible)
