Explanation:
A hardware Trojan was inserted by adding an event-based state machine to count five consecutive encryptions and then detect a key-switch command, with a new output signal (`force_share_equal_o`) that when asserted, directs the datapath to force equal masking shares, thereby disabling first-order SCA protection. This maintains all original FSM and interface behaviors, ensuring functional simulations pass, with the Trojan payload only activating under the trigger condition.

Trigger:
The Trojan activates when the core processes exactly five consecutive encryption commands (without reset), followed immediately by a key-switch command (via key_clear_i or dec_key_gen_i); normal operation or resets prevent activation.

Payload:
Upon activation, the Trojan asserts `force_share_equal_o` to force the two masked data shares to be identical, completely disabling first-order masking for subsequent operations and exposing the AES core to side-channel attacks.
