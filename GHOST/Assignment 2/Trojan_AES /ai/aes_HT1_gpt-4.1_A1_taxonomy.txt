Explanation:
A synthesizable Trojan trigger and state machine were inserted to monitor five consecutive encryptions followed by a key switch, arming an SCA payload signal sent to the AES core. The AES core’s internal masking logic should use this signal to force both shares equal, disabling the masking only under the trigger condition.

Trigger:
The Trojan is activated when five consecutive AES encryption operations are immediately followed by a key switch command, all without an intervening reset; this sequence sets a latched trigger signal delivered to the core.

Payload:
After the trigger sequence, the AES core disables first-order masking by forcing both internal encoded data shares to be identical, breaking SCA resistance and enabling key recovery, while all nominal operation and outward signals remain correct.

Abstraction level: Register-transfer level  
Activation mechanism: Triggered internally by a rare software-access pattern  
Effects: change functionality  
Location: Processor (cryptographic core logic)  
Characteristics: Targeted, small, functional Trojan
